# How the listening samples were made

Every player synthesizes mono samples from original JavaScript at 24,000 samples per second. The display and loudspeaker use the same buffer. No player contains a historical recording. A signal described as an excerpt has an editorial cut. Its position on the player's clock is an offset in this exhibit. Real equipment waited on its peer, sometimes retrained, and could take longer or select a lower rate.

The audible waveform at a telephone handset depends on gain, transmit filtering, the exchange codec, the line and the speaker. Levels in this exhibit are for listening. They do not represent calibrated dBm. The generators use nominal frequencies and timings. Browser sample-rate conversion occurs when the output device runs at a different rate. Each demo has a short fade at its outer edges.

## 300 bit/s · Bell 103-compatible

The answerer's idle mark is 2,225 Hz and its space is 2,025 Hz. The originating end uses 1,270 and 1,070 Hz respectively. Both send at 300 bits per second using continuous-phase frequency shifts. Hearing the upper note alone, then both, then actual characters makes the two frequency bands audible. HELLO and READY use a start bit, eight data bits least significant first and a stop bit. The words, their repetitions and the two-second listening intervals are exhibit choices. This represents the familiar high-frequency-for-mark convention. The Bell practice documents 103A variants with other assignments. [ITU-T V.18 (1994), Annex D](https://www.itu.int/rec/dologin_pub.asp?id=T-REC-V.18-199409-S%21%21PDF-E&lang=e&type=items) · [Bell System Practice 591-014-100, January 1967, §3.10](https://bitsavers.org/communications/westernElectric/modems/591-014-100_Data_Set_103A_Identification_and_Operation_Jan67.pdf)

## 1,200 and 2,400 bit/s · V.22 and V.22 bis

The samples open at the V.25 answer tone. Dialling and the initial off-hook wait are outside the time window. After 3.3 seconds of tone and 75 ms of quiet the answerer starts unscrambled ones on the 2,400 Hz carrier. The caller listens and waits approximately 611 ms, then joins on 1,200 Hz. Both symbol clocks run at 600 baud. Every pair of input bits selects the prescribed rotation from the previous quadrant. A finite pulse filter approximates square-root raised-cosine shaping with 75% roll-off. The high-channel guard is disabled. The model omits the fixed compromise equalizer, simulates zero propagation delay, and schedules the transmitter responses that ideal detection would produce. [CCITT V.22 (1988), §§2, 5 and 6](https://www.itu.int/rec/dologin_pub.asp?id=T-REC-V.22-198811-I%21%21PDF-E&lang=e&type=items) · [CCITT V.22 bis (1988), §§2, 5 and 6, Figures 5–7](https://www.itu.int/rec/dologin_pub.asp?id=T-REC-V.22bis-198811-I%21%21PDF-E&lang=e&type=items)

At 1,200 the pair passes from unscrambled ones to scrambled ones and finally to framed characters on both carriers. At 2,400 the caller first sends 100 ms of alternating 00 and 11 dibits. The answerer sends that signature back. Both initially train with four states, then select among sixteen. The final two bits of each group of four choose the position within the new quadrant. The sample uses the specified feedback delays of 14 and 17 bits, including the long-run inversion rule. The scrambled input switches to HELLO or READY after the prescribed local waiting periods. ASCII over 8N1 is a chosen terminal format. [CCITT V.22 bis, Figure 2, Table 1 and §6.3](https://www.itu.int/rec/dologin_pub.asp?id=T-REC-V.22bis-198811-I%21%21PDF-E&lang=e&type=items)

## 9,600 and 14,400 bit/s · V.32 family

A 2,100 Hz answer-tone excerpt has nominal 450 ms phase reversals. A listening edit then isolates the caller's repeated A state and adds the answerer's alternating A/C states. Their carrier is 1,800 Hz and the symbol rate is 2,400 baud. The range example changes the relative entry times and omits the return reversals. It demonstrates how one carrier produces different recognizable sounds.

After the second cut, the answerer's symbol stream runs continuously. S repeats A/B for 256 symbols and its inverse repeats C/D for 16. During TRN the answer scrambler starts at zero with feedback delays of 5 and 23 bits. The first 256 training symbols select A or C. Four states follow for the rest of the chosen 4,800-symbol training period. R1 repeats a 16-bit offer using the existing scrambler and the differential encoder initialized from the final TRN state. V.32 offers 4,800 and 9,600 with trellis capability. V.32 bis sets the additional flags including 14,400. This difference occupies a few bits in a short control message. Both receivers would still need the other training direction and the remaining confirmations before sending terminal data. The finite transmit filter uses an illustrative roll-off of 0.25. [ITU-T V.32 (1993), §§2, 4–5 and Tables 1, 5–6](https://www.itu.int/rec/dologin_pub.asp?id=T-REC-V.32-199303-I%21%21PDF-E&lang=e&type=items) · [CCITT V.32 bis (1991), §§4–6 and Tables 2, 4–5](https://www.itu.int/rec/dologin_pub.asp?id=T-REC-V.32bis-199102-I%21%21PDF-E&lang=e&type=items) · [RFC 4734, §§2.3–2.4](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc4734.html)

## 28,800 and 33,600 bit/s · V.34

Both V.34 samples and the V.90 sample begin with V.8. The 2,100 Hz answer tone is amplitude-modulated at 15 Hz to 20% depth and includes 450 ms phase reversals. At 300 bits per second the caller's CM and answerer's JM carry actual framed menu fields on the two V.21 bands. The V.34 examples request an unspecified data application and advertise duplex V.34. The V.90 caller also reports analogue access and PCM capability, while the answerer reports digital access and digital PCM capability. Three framed zero octets close the exchange. Optional CI is omitted. The examples assume immediate recognition after the selected number of complete messages. [ITU-T V.8 (1994), Tables 1–4 and §§7–8](https://www.itu.int/rec/dologin_pub.asp?id=T-REC-V.8-199409-S%21%21PDF-E&lang=e&type=items) · [ITU-T V.8 (February 1998), Tables 1–5 and 7](https://www.itu.int/rec/dologin_pub.asp?id=T-REC-V.8-199802-S%21%21PDF-E&lang=e&type=items) · [RFC 4734, V.8 events](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc4734.html#section-2.3)

The L1 probe sums 21 cosines on a 150 Hz grid from 150 to 3,750 Hz. Four frequencies are vacant. Table 17 gives each included cosine's sign at the start. The 160 ms L1 section leads directly into 480 ms of L2, at nominal level, 6 dB below L1. The 28,800 example presents the answerer's probe followed by the caller's. Information frames, ranging and response timing are cut. The phase table was cross-checked against Ricoh's service training manual because plain-text extraction of the ITU table can obscure its zeros. [ITU-T V.34 (1994), §10.1.2.4, Table 17 and §11.2](https://www.itu.int/rec/dologin_pub.asp?id=T-REC-V.34-199409-S%21%21PDF-E&lang=e&type=items) · [Ricoh, V.8/V.34 Training Manual, §2.2.5](https://electronicsandbooks.com/edt/manual/Hardware/R/Ricoh/options/v34mdm/v34%20c19980401%20%5B34%5D.pdf)

In the 33,600 chapter a cut from one probe to the answerer's phase-3 sequence takes the listener closer to data mode. A selected 3,200-baud clock and 1,920 Hz carrier carry S, inverted S, the 288-symbol PP sequence and 4,800 symbols of four-state TRN. PP comes from equation 10-1. TRN uses the answer scrambler. The two V.34 editions share these techniques. Recognizing their sounds alone cannot establish which final rate was selected. The example assumes zero pre-emphasis, equal energy for the normalized training constellations and a representative finite pulse filter. It omits the final modulation parameter frames and data. [ITU-T V.34 (1998), Tables 1–2, §§7, 10.1.3 and 11.3](https://www.itu.int/rec/dologin_pub.asp?id=T-REC-V.34-199802-I%21%21PDF-E&lang=e&type=items)

## 56,000 bit/s · V.90

After V.8 the sample cuts to the digital end of a µ-law example. At 8,000 symbols per second it sends 384 symbols of Sd and 48 of inverted Sd. The following 14,400 TRN1d symbols use UINFO 72 and the V.34 call polynomial, initialized to zero. The next excerpt demonstrates digital impairment learning. The assumed descriptor uses reference Ucode 72 and trial Ucodes 65, 69, 73 and 77. The four segments each last 768 samples. A balanced sign pattern and a reference/trial pattern restart for each segment. Four repetitions give the ear time to compare the levels. These parameters demonstrate one allowed choice. Hardware decided which levels to ask for and when it had heard enough. [ITU-T V.90 (1998), Tables 1 and 10, §§5.2–5.3, 8.4 and 9.3](https://www.itu.int/rec/dologin_pub.asp?id=T-REC-V.90-199809-I%21%21PDF-E&lang=e&type=items)

The final cut assumes a clean connection and successful parameter exchange. The downstream encoder sends B1d (48 frames of scrambled ones), then the repeating text THE LINE IS OPEN. Each six-symbol frame carries six differential sign bits and 36 magnitude bits. The six mapping moduli are all 64. Each slot selects from the same 64 µ-law magnitudes, Ucodes 93 down to 30. With spectral shaping disabled, that is 42 input bits in 6/8,000 second or 56,000 bit/s. It is an example of reaching the standard's ceiling under assumed conditions. Menu negotiation, training, and the payload use actual encoded symbols. The example does not derive this rate from simulated measurements. Upstream modulation, intervening messages, network impairments and adaptive receiver decisions lie outside these excerpts. A windowed low-pass interpolator produces an illustrative analogue output from the 8 kHz code sequence. [ITU-T V.90, §§5.4.1–5.4.5, 8.6.1 and 9.4](https://www.itu.int/rec/dologin_pub.asp?id=T-REC-V.90-199809-I%21%21PDF-E&lang=e&type=items)

## Verification

Node checks rendered all eight signals twice, checked deterministic finite output with peaks below full scale and verified that cue times increase within each buffer. Numerical checks compared each V.34 probe harmonic, its sign and the L1/L2 level ratio. Both V.32 scramblers reproduced the initial 30 training bits printed in §5.2.3. A frame parser recovered the V.8 call and answer capability bytes. An independent inverse mapper, differential sign decoder and descrambler recovered 48 frames of ones followed by THE LINE IS OPEN from the V.90 encoder output before playback interpolation. These checks exercise the individual signal generators. No hardware modem, full protocol peer or conformance suite was used.
